### DEVIATION from the brief, stated plainly

The brief names `Wan2_2-Animate-14B_fp8_e4m3fn_scaled_KJ.safetensors` by exact filename.
That file was **not** downloaded. Kijai's own `Wan22Animate/README.md` in the same repo

can keep using the initial models." The brief also requires the **native** graph. The two
instructions cannot both be honoured, so the v2 of the **same precision** (e4m3fn) was
taken: same repo, same licence, one precision only (the no-two-precisions rule holds).

`wan_animate_service.py` mirrors `wan_video_service._submit_wan_clip`'s call shape
(reference still, pose folder, face folder, mode, frames, seed, LoRA) and adds
`generate_animate_clips()` for the chained long render.

### The inputs are ours; neither trap is wired

* **No DWPose anywhere.** The tutorial's own preprocessor is banned here. The pose video
  is drawn per frame by `build_pose_frames()` from `geom.keypoints.detect` (MediaPipe),
  in the **same** OpenPose colour and bone convention as `geom/char_vace.py` — the
  constants `_BONES` and `_POSE_COLOURS` are *imported from that module*, so the VACE
  control channel and this engine cannot drift into describing the same body two
  different ways.
* **No FLUX.1-Kontext retarget.** Not wired, and the FLUX.1-dev family is not on this box.
* `build_pose_frames()` prefers S08's `output/pose_tracks/<id>/track.json` when it exists
  (`_track_frame_pts` reads it defensively and falls back to MediaPipe for anything it
  does not recognise). S08's track had not landed, so this run is MediaPipe.

### Chunk seams: 81 and **157**, not 81 and 162

Read off `comfy_extras/nodes_wan.py` rather than assumed. `WanAnimateToVideo` slices
`pose_video`/`face_video` at `video_frame_offset`, and with `continue_motion` it first
does `offset -= len(continue_motion)`, then returns `trim_image = max(0,
ref_motion_latent_length*4 - 3)`. With `continue_motion_max_frames = 5` (the node
default) that is `trim_image = 5`, so chunk 0 yields 81 new frames and every later chunk
yields 76. A three-chunk render is 81 + 76 + 76 = **233** frames with seams at output
frames **81 and 157**. The brief's "81 and 162" is what naive non-overlapping chunking
would give; the service returns the real indices so no receipt has to guess them.

### The driver is arm (i), and that is deliberate

Arm (i) — the current VACE static-skeleton render of this shot — is also the driver for
arms (ii)–(iv). The alternative was to drive from the sibling render `scene20_v20`, but
that measured **34.81 dB PSNR** against `scene20_v29` on the identical crop at frame 120:
the same base render with a different grade, so it is not an independent baseline and
using it as one would have been a dressed-up version of the same circularity. Driving
from arm (i) means every arm sees identical motion, identical framing and identical
length; the only thing that differs is where the identity comes from. Arm (ii) is **not**
scored against its own conditioning image (see below).

### The anchor had to be changed, and the reason is a measurement

The house canon anchor is photoreal FLUX; this shot and every arm are painterly
("arcane") renders. Scored against the photoreal canon, arm (i) came out at
**mean 0.0998** (min 0.0575, max 0.1535) — while Abel's photoreal canon scores **0.2214**
against *Cornelis's* photoreal canon. The same person across the style gap ranked BELOW a
different person within one style. A scorer in that state decides nothing, and reporting
its table would have been a confident, meaningless number.

Full pairwise calibration in `output/animate_receipts/anchor_calibration.txt` (18/18 faces
detected):

* Abel's photoreal canon vs Abel's own arcane cells: **0.011 – 0.142**
* Abel's arcane cells vs each other: **0.257 – 0.814**
* Abel's arcane cells vs Cornelis's arcane cells: **−0.003 – 0.225**

So the anchor is an **in-style** image: `arcane_prev_v5/cell_06.png`. It is deliberately
*not* the cell used as arm (ii)'s reference still — `cell_00.png` conditions the render,
`cell_06.png` scores it, and the two sit 0.649 apart (plainly the same person,
independently drawn).

### The gate

Judged on the **step-matched** arm (ii-25), because that is the only version of arm (ii)
that is not handicapped against arm (i):

| leg | result |
|---|---|
| (ii) beats (i) | **FAIL** — 0.2338 vs 0.3245, **−0.0907** (at 12 steps: −0.0676) |
| (ii) beats (iii) | **PASS** — 0.2338 vs 0.0332, **+0.2006** (at 12 steps: +0.2237) |
| overall | **FAIL** |

Arm (iii) at 0.0332 sits far below even the N1 different-person floor of 0.1678: handed
the wrong reference, the engine draws someone who is not Abel and is not Cornelis either.
That is the arm doing its job — it proves the identity in arm (ii) comes from the
reference still and not from the driver, the pose, or the prompt.

### Arm (iv) is a check on the scorer, and it passes

Per-frame cosine is order-invariant, so a shuffle **must** leave min/mean/spread
untouched. It does: 0.1886 / 0.2569 / 0.1310 in both arms, to four decimals. The scorer
is measuring per-frame identity and nothing else.

Its real job is to be the null for the **seam** statistic:

| seam (output frame) | (ii) 12 steps | (ii-25) 25 steps | arm (iv) shuffled null | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 81 | 0.0185 (signed −) | 0.0145 (signed −) | 0.0198 | **not distinguishable from noise** |
| 157 | **0.0513** (signed −) | **0.0539** (signed −) | 0.0196 | **real**, 2.6–2.7× the null |

Seam drift is `|mean of the 8 frames before − mean of the 8 after|`, 8 scored frames on
each side of both seams. The first hand-over costs nothing measurable; the second costs
0.05 of cosine and the sign is negative both times — identity only ever degrades across a
seam, it never recovers.

### Per-chunk means: Animate decays, the static-skeleton render does not

| chunk (frames) | (ii) 12 steps | (ii-25) 25 steps | (i) VACE |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (0–80) | 0.2662 | 0.2485 | 0.3147 |
| 1 (81–156) | 0.2674 | 0.2500 | 0.3280 |
| 2 (157–232) | **0.2366** | **0.2021** | 0.3313 |

This is the more useful finding than the headline. Arm (ii) holds flat across the first
hand-over and then loses 0.031 across the second, while the current path drifts *upward*

what leaks.

## 6. What the numbers do not say, and one defect they hide

Two confounds, both stated because they weigh **against** arm (ii):

1. ~~**Steps.**~~ **Closed by measurement.** The VACE arm was rendered at **25 steps**
   (read out of `logbook_scene20_v29.jsonl`) and the first Animate pass ran at **12**,
   chosen because five sessions were serialised on one GPU lock. Arm (ii) was therefore
   re-rendered at 25 steps. It came out **worse**, not better: 0.2338 against 0.2569.
   More sampling does not buy identity here, so the step gap was not what produced the
   failure and the negative result stands on its own.
2. **Arm (i) is also the driver.** Justified above, but it means arm (i) is being scored
   on footage it generated itself, which is the most favourable possible position for it.

And the defect the mean cosine hides entirely: **Animate replaces the scene, it does not
preserve it.** `background_video` is an optional input on node 30 and this graph does not
wire it, so the model has to invent everything outside the character from the reference
still's plain studio backdrop. By eye at frames 40 and 120 the face and doublet are
clean and correct, while the table objects render as blue-teal blobs and the left hand is
a black smear. A face-identity metric scores that as a success. It is not one. Wiring
`background_video` to the driver frames is the first thing the next session should try.
