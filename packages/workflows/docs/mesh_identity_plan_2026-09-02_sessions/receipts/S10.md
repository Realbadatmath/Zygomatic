## 2. What the tail actually did

`reach_max` is the profile's biggest lobe: **sphere 1.413 = no tail, anchor 1.674 = tail.**

| arm | side reach_max | what the render shows at the anchor yaw |
|---|---|---|
| MV | **1.599** | a large **segmented** tail, curling up and back, right place, right scale |
| N0 | 1.582 | a tail, but **malformed** — split/crumpled where the anchor's is a clean coil |
| N1 | 1.444 | barely above sphere; a smooth taper on the front view, **no tail at the side** |
| TRELLIS | 1.497 | a ball with a stub — **no tail as geometry**, as found on 09-01 |
| procedural | 1.836 | the real tail |

**MV is the only arm that produced a correctly placed, correctly scaled, segmented tail.**
Mouth: present and open on MV, N0 and N1 (visual, at the corrected front yaw).

These two rows are a **visual** verdict on `figures/anchor_yaws_all_arms.png`, not a gate.
`reach_max` alone does NOT separate MV from N0 (1.599 vs 1.582) even though the renders
differ obviously — the profile distance does (1.077 vs 1.609). Anyone building a tail gate
should use the profile, not the lobe height.

**N1 grew a face on more than one side.** In `figures/ring_yaw_check.png` the N1 mesh shows
eyes and a mouth at ring azimuths 0, 90, 180 AND 270. That is why N1 scores *best of the
three arms on the front view* (0.942) — a mesh that is a face everywhere matches a face
anchor from anywhere. **The front number in isolation is misleading and should not be
quoted on its own.** Feeding a mirrored view does not merely waste the slot; it actively
teaches the model a wrong symmetry.

## 3. A measurement error I made, and the correction

The first pass compared the front anchor against a **rear view** of MV and N0. `render_mesh.py`
(inherited from `mesh_trellis/render_pair.py`) normalises translation and scale but never
**rotation**, and Pixal3D's exported frame does not agree with the procedural mesh's: MV and
N0 put their face at ring azimuth 180, procedural and TRELLIS at 0. Visible immediately in
`figures/unaligned_first_pass.png`. Those first numbers were meaningless, not merely noisy.

The fix is one yaw offset per mesh. **My first attempt at it failed and was thrown away**:
`score_aligned.py` picked the offset by minimising the reach distance to the front anchor,
and it put the procedural mesh — which visibly faces 0 — at **315°**, making its side score
*worse* (1.744 vs 0.742). I had written the acceptance test into that file before running it
("procedural and trellis must come out near 0; if they do not, the alignment is wrong, not
the meshes"), so it was refused on its own terms.

`find_front.py` then ran two detectors against those same nulls:

| detector | procedural | trellis | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| reach-to-front-anchor | 315° (err 45°) | 0° | **REFUSED** |
| DINOv2 CLS cosine | **0°** | **338°** (err 22°, one ring step) | **USABLE** |

The DINOv2 offsets were then checked by eye and agree on all three meshes whose orientation
is visible (MV 180, N0 180, procedural 0). Side view read at front+270 with **no search**,
except that the *choice of profile* (+90 vs +270) is min'd exactly as `reach.py` already
min's over the mirror — applied identically to every arm including the nulls, because
picking handedness by hand is how two surfaces end up disagreeing about a convention.
Quantisation is one ring step, 22.5°.

## 4. The view-rank metric is REFUSED — its null says it has no power

Brief asked for DINOv2 view-rank of the true view among 16 renders, chance 8.5. It ran:

| arm | rank | best-matching azimuth |
|---|---|---|
| MV | 4/16 | 135° |
| N0 | 8/16 | 158° |
| N1 | 11/16 | 202° |
| TRELLIS | **1/16** | 270° |
| **procedural** | **6/16** | **338°** |

**Do not use these numbers.** The procedural mesh *is* the creature, and the instrument ranks
its true yaw 6th of 16 against a chance of 8.5 — barely better than a coin. Meanwhile TRELLIS
ranks 1st, and TRELLIS is the mesh independently measured here to have no tail geometry at
all. The ordering is tracking baked appearance, not orientation. A metric on which the right
answer scores near chance and a known-wrong answer scores perfect cannot certify MV's rank 4.
Recorded as **not_detectable**, not as a result.

Note the asymmetry that makes this consistent: DINOv2 against the **front** anchor passed its
nulls and is used for alignment (§3); DINOv2 against the **side** anchor failed them. The
same encoder is usable for one question and useless for the other, which is exactly why each
use needs its own null rather than one blanket judgement about the encoder.

## 7. Install notes — two of my own calls were wrong

- **"natten is not needed" was WRONG.** My grep covered the clone, where natten never appears.
  It arrives at **runtime via `torch.hub`**: Pixal3D's NAF upsampler (`valeoai/NAF`) does
  `from natten.functional import na2d_av, na2d_qk`. A clone-only grep cannot see a torch.hub
  dependency. Resolved with the **prebuilt 0.17.5 cp312 wheel** from `whl.natten.org`; NAF has
  an explicit legacy branch that accepts it. natten 0.21 (what the README asks for) has **no
  prebuilt cp312 wheel** and needs a source build.
- **"there is no nvcc on this box" was WRONG.** There is a complete **CUDA 12.4 toolkit at
  `/home/zygomatic/TRELLIS.2/cudatk`** — it is simply not on PATH. With `CUDA_HOME` pointed at
  it, `torch_scatter` and `torch_cluster` build and import fine. The pip `nvidia-cuda-nvcc-cu12`
  package is **not** a substitute: it ships `ptxas` but no `nvcc` binary.
- **flash_attn**: the prebuilt release wheels are unusable — they need a **cxx11-ABI torch**,
  and the `download.pytorch.org/whl/cu124` wheel is old-ABI (`libc10.so` exports `...ErrorC2...Ss`,
  not `__cxx11`). Both the abiTRUE and abiFALSE wheels fail identically. `flash-attn<3` resolves
  to 2.8.x which **fails to compile**; **2.7.4.post1 builds cleanly** against `cudatk`.
- `data.pyg.org` is **unreachable** from this box (http 000), so PyG prebuilt wheels are not an
  option — source builds are the only route.
- Pixal3D peaks at **46.9 GB VRAM** on a 47 GB card in the 1536 cascade. **`--low_vram` alone
  silently drops the pipeline from `1536_cascade` to `1024_cascade`** — it must be paired with
  `--resolution 1536` or the arm is not comparable. N1's first attempt was OOM-killed by
  concurrent sessions; the rerun used `--low_vram --resolution 1536` (30.0 GB peak, same cascade).
